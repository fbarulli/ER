## 3. Every model ranks *worse than chance* here — and negatives outrank positives

| track | median positive | median negative | separation | AUC (real pairs, n=22+19) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline (frozen MiniLM) | — | — | — | 0.419 |
| A text | 0.8674 | 0.9434 | **−0.0761** | 0.462 |
| B gnn_only | 0.7045 | 0.7085 | **−0.0039** | 0.768 |
| C hybrid | 0.6653 | 0.7106 | **−0.0453** | 0.615 |

* **3 of 4 positives fall below the median negative for all three tracks (75 %).**
* Negatives above the median positive: text 4/6, gnn_only 4/6, hybrid 5/6.

**Answer to "did the models struggle with positives more than negatives?"** — both
sides fail, and the asymmetry runs the *wrong* way for positives: the negative
cloud sits **above** the positive cloud, so the errors are not missed positives
but **over-scored negatives that drag the positives down**. Recall and precision
both collapse together, which is why F1 never exceeds 0.57 at any threshold.

## 4. Calibration is the most broken part of the run

### 4.1 The fitted threshold sits outside the positive distribution

| track | fitted threshold | positives above it (real+real_bundle, n=202) | accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 0.9947 | **0 / 202** | 0.262 |
| text | 0.9942 | **0 / 202** | 0.262 |
| gnn_only | 0.7307 | 109 / 202 | 0.996 |
| hybrid | 0.7299 | **1 / 202** | 0.262 |

Accuracy 0.262 is exactly the negative rate — at their fitted thresholds,
baseline/text/hybrid **classify everything as a non-match**.

`dev_youden` fitted on 4 positives / 6 negatives picks the single highest-scoring
pair as the operating point: precision 1.000, recall 0.250, F1 0.400 for all
three tracks. That is a degenerate threshold, not a calibrated one.

### 4.2 The declared operating points do not exist on the graph tracks' scale

| threshold | text | gnn_only | hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| fitted (dev_youden) | P 1.000 / R 0.250 | P 1.000 / R 0.250 | P 1.000 / R 0.250 |
| `balanced_review` 0.787 | P 0.333 / R 0.750 | **no predictions** | **no predictions** |
| `high_precision` 0.850 | P 0.286 / R 0.500 | **no predictions** | **no predictions** |
| `operating_*` 0.950 | P 0.500 / R 0.250 | **no predictions** | **no predictions** |
| `fixed_threshold` 0.550 | P 0.400 / R 1.000 | P 0.400 / R 1.000 | P 0.400 / R 1.000 |

The graph tracks' scores top out at ≈0.73, so every operating point declared in
`config/training.yaml` (0.787 / 0.85 / 0.95) is **unreachable**. Only the
0.55 fallback fires, and it predicts everything positive. This is a config/scale
mismatch, not a model failure, and it is a one-line-per-track fix.

### 4.3 The score is not a usable probability

Expected calibration error, treating the score as P(match):

| track | ECE |
|---|---|
| text | **0.578** |
| gnn_only | 0.300 |
| hybrid | 0.292 |

Text's calibration is *monotonically inverted*: its lowest score bin (mean 0.778)
has positive rate **1.000**, and its highest bin (mean 0.975) has positive rate
0.333. Higher score means *less* likely to match.

per-epoch dev metrics, which the repo config already requests.

## 7. Embedding geometry and clusters

Method note: my first pass used connected components on a thresholded cosine
graph. That is **single-linkage chaining**, which manufactures one giant blob and
overstated the collapse. The numbers below use a **mutual-kNN graph +
greedy-modularity communities** on the same embeddings.

| track | dim | cos mean | cos p90 | communities @k=5 | ARI vs GTIN | ARI vs brand |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| text | 384 | 0.454 | 0.584 | 47 | **+0.070** | **+0.271** |
| gnn_only | 128 | 0.545 | 0.726 | 43 | **+0.042** | +0.171 |
| hybrid | 128 | 0.436 | 0.667 | 34 | **+0.030** | +0.106 |

Conclusions:

1. **No track's embedding space recovers product identity.** ARI vs GTIN is
   0.03–0.07 for every track. The biggest communities contain *one listing per
   GTIN* (e.g. 21 listings / 21 distinct GTINs) — they are semantic
   neighbourhoods, not identity clusters.
2. **NMI vs GTIN reads ≈0.78 and must not be trusted** — on a catalog of
   mostly-distinct GTINs both labellings are near-unique, so NMI rewards the
   trivial agreement. ARI is the honest metric.
3. **The clusters track brand, not identity** (ARI vs brand 0.27 for text).
   That is exactly why text retrieves best (recall@1 0.875 vs 0.375 gnn / 0.250
   hybrid): same-brand listings in this catalog are usually the same product
   across retailers.
4. **gnn_only has the most compressed geometry** (cos mean 0.545, p90 0.726) and
   the worst retrieval (0.375) despite the best pair-scorer AUC (0.768).
   **The pair scorer works; the embedding does not.**

### Which clustering method to use

* **Not** single-linkage connected components — chaining is why the first pass
  read as total collapse.
* **For measuring structure:** mutual-kNN graph + modularity communities, scored
  with ARI/NMI against identity, plus the silhouette and the component-size
  distribution. This is what the dashboard now uses.
* **For retrieval (what the product actually does):** HNSW
  (`hnsw_m 16`, `ef_construction 200`, `ef_search 100`). The repo does **not**
  cluster — identity is resolved later by thresholded matching and the RandIndex
  assignment. So "the clusters the GNN created" is a diagnostic, not a pipeline
  stage.

## 8. Why hybrid underperforms text — the fusion weight

| track | graph cosine weight | text cosine weight | bias |
|---|---|---|---|
| gnn_only | 0.999 | — | −0.001 |
| hybrid | **0.499** | **0.499** | −0.001 |

Hybrid averages a strong learned graph signal with the **frozen baseline** text
signal (baseline AUC 0.419). A 50/50 mix of a 0.77 signal and a 0.42 signal lands
at 0.615 — worse than either the graph alone or the fine-tuned text alone. The
weight is a fixed equal split, not a fitted one, and it is the single clearest
defect in the track design.

## 12. Conclusions

1. **The headline metrics from this run are unusable as a model comparison.** Ten
   scored pairs, AUC confidence intervals spanning [0.00, 0.88], test reporting
   off. Every model is statistically indistinguishable from chance on the
   population the run reports.
2. **The scored population is a pack-size discrimination task**, and it is
   perfectly labelled. Text's ceiling here is structural (the signal is one
   numeral in the title); the graph tracks own that signal but score it through a
   fusion weight that drags them below chance.
3. **Calibration, not discrimination, is the loudest failure.** Three of four
   models put zero positives above their own fitted threshold. The declared
   operating points (0.787/0.85/0.95) are unreachable on the graph tracks' score
   scale.
4. **Hybrid's equal-weight fusion is a design defect**: it mixes a 0.77 signal
   with a 0.42 signal and lands at 0.615.
5. **No collapse, no overfitting evidence, no usable slice data.** The run is
   simply too small (3 optimizer steps) to answer those questions.
6. **The scoring population is dominated by pack/volume**, the same axis the
   models are weakest on collectively, and the attribute-ablation shows
   `count per unit` and `volume` are among the *least* used features per unit of
   score change while `sweetener` dominates — the opposite of what this
   population needs.

## 13. Objectives for the next run

Ordered by expected information gain. Each is measurable on the artifact that
already exists.

### O1 — Make the measurement capable of deciding anything (blocking)
* **Target:** test-fold scored population ≥ 300 pairs (currently 10); bootstrap
  CI half-width on ROC-AUC ≤ 0.10 (currently ≈0.40).
* **How:** `report_test: true` is already committed; the cohort must grow past
  10k or the scored slice must stop being a pack-size-only sample.
* **Evidence:** `*__report_manifest.json → confidence_intervals`.

### O2 — Fix the calibration contract per track
* **Target:** every track's fitted threshold puts ≥50 % of test positives above
  it, and precision/recall at the fitted threshold both ≥0.60 on the test fold.
* **How:** per-track operating points on each track's own score scale (the graph
  tracks do not reach 0.787 today); `dev_youden` must be replaced or guarded
  against a degenerate 1-positive fit.
* **Evidence:** `/compare` calibration table + ECE.

### O3 — Make hybrid's fusion fitted, not fixed
* **Target:** hybrid AUC ≥ max(text, gnn_only) on the test fold; today it is
  below both.
* **How:** learn or calibrate `graph_cosine_weight` / `text_cosine_weight` per
  track instead of the hard-coded 0.499/0.499.
* **Evidence:** `*__epoch_metrics.jsonl → scorer_calibration`.

### O4 — Give the graph encoder a retrieval-usable geometry
* **Target:** ARI vs GTIN ≥ 0.30 at k=5 (today 0.042), and recall@1 ≥ 0.70 for
  gnn_only (today 0.375).
* **How:** the graph loss is classification + cosine metric only — there is no
  repulsion/uniformity term, so nothing pushes listings apart. Add one, or train
  the retriever with a contrastive objective over the listing graph.
* **Evidence:** `/compare` embedding-geometry table.

### O5 — Fix the pack/volume axis explicitly
* **Target:** on the pack-blocker subset, AUC ≥ 0.85 (today ≈0.4–0.5).
* **How:** `count per unit` is the weakest live feature (|Δ| 0.0002–0.0007).
  It is a numeric graph field; either up-weight it or give the text channel an
  explicit normalized pack/volume token (it already appends one for volume, not
  for count).
* **Evidence:** ablation rows where `gate_reason` starts with `pack_blocker`.

### O6 — Train each track with a different loss
* **Target:** a per-track loss assignment, measured not assumed.
* **How:** `--loss {mnrl,contrastive,triplet}` already exists and
  `training.loss` is one global value. Assign per track, and record which loss
  produced which axis: text needs in-batch ranking, the graph scorer needs a
  margin on hard negatives, hybrid needs whatever survives the fusion.
* **Evidence:** one run per `(track, loss)`; compare on O1's population.

### O7 — Populate the slices or stop claiming them
* **Target:** `sparse_neighborhood` and `isolated` non-empty; every declared
  `slice_column` populated on the cohort.
* **Evidence:** `*__slice_metrics.csv`; today both are 0 rows and every
  `masking_profile`/`generation_variant` is null.

### O8 — Multi-embedding instead of one fused vector
* **Rationale from this run:** only 7 of 37 attributes carry signal, they drive
  *different* decisions (sweetener/carbonation for identity, volume/count for
  size), and the single fused vector forces one geometry to serve all of them —
  which is why no track's geometry recovers identity (§7) and why hybrid's
  average is worse than its parts (§8).
* **Proposal:** one embedding per decision axis — (a) size/volume axis,
  (b) flavour/sweetener axis, (c) brand/category axis — plus the fused vector
  retained for compatibility. Retrieval runs per axis and the axes vote; a
  missing attribute degrades only its own axis instead of the whole vector.
* **Target to justify it:** per-axis retrieval recall@1 ≥ the fused vector's, and
  hybrid ≥ max(parts) on the test fold.
